Synaptic Plasticity as the Physical Substrate of the Self

How changing connections in the brain may support memory, personality, identity, and consciousness.

Interactive reading edition | Jack N. | August 2026

Abstract

Synaptic plasticity is the brain's ability to change the strength and organization of communication between neurons. This report argues that plasticity is a major physical support for memory and personal continuity, while avoiding the claim that one synapse or one brain region contains the self.

Central question: If experience changes the brain's connections, and those connections help preserve memory and behavior, how much of the self is physically represented in the brain?

The visual organizes the evidence; it does not add a new statistical result.

How the models connect to the evidence

Distributed biology

The Allen Human Brain Atlas map shows that synaptic and identity-relevant genes vary across cortical regions. That pattern supports a distributed account of memory and identity rather than a single storage location.

Pathology and cognition

The SEA-AD analysis connects pathological burden with cognitive decline, especially in memory, language, and overall cognition. The live model gives those relationships a spatial frame without pretending that a visualization can replace a dataset.

Plasticity and lived function

Classic LTP and LTD experiments, human synaptic PET, tau progression studies, Parkinson's plasticity results, and memory-prosthesis experiments all point to the same careful conclusion: synaptic change is a central component of brain function, but it is not a complete code for a person.

Interpretation rule: The models are explanatory interfaces. They show scale, location, and relationships that help a reader understand the argument; they are not patient-specific diagnosis, treatment guidance, or proof of causation.

Conclusion

Synaptic patterns are a necessary part of personal continuity, but the self is an emergent process involving whole-brain networks, the body, development, and relationships. The live models make that claim inspectable: the viewer can move from tumor scale to the body, from the body to the brain, and from the brain to the synapse while keeping the limits of the evidence in view.
